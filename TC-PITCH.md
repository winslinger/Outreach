## Pain Points by Industry

### Fashion & Apparel (CEOs, Founders)

**What they spend money on:** Seasonal shoots (4× per year minimum), lookbook production, campaign imagery, social content, influencer shoots.

**What it costs them:** A single fashion shoot day runs $5k–$20k+ (photographer, model, stylist, studio, retouching). Multiply by 4 seasons and multiple SKUs.

**What they never have enough of:** Content for social. They shoot 2 days a year and need 365 days of content.

**TC angle:** We can deliver a full seasonal campaign — 20–30 campaign-quality images and 2–3 videos — at a fraction of a single shoot day. Style-matched to their existing brand aesthetic. As many SKU variants as they want.

**Opening hooks:**
- Seasonal content pressure: "Most fashion brands run 4 shoots a year and still never have enough content."
- Cost angle: "Studio shoots are the most expensive part of running a product brand."
- Scale angle: "The brands that win on social publish 10x more content than they can afford to shoot traditionally."

### Gadgets & Wearables (CEOs, Founders, CTOs)

**What they spend money on:** Product launch shoots, lifestyle integration photography, tech demo videos, review content.

**What it costs them:** Getting a wearable or gadget to look premium requires controlled environments, specific lighting setups, skilled product photographers. Videos with real environments (mountain running, swimming, travel) need location shoots.

**What they never have enough of:** Environment variety — same product, 20 different contexts. Traditional production can't afford it.

**TC angle:** We can generate the same product in 20 different environments — lifestyle, extreme sport, travel, urban — all brand-consistent, all at scale. No location shoots required.

**Opening hooks:**
- Environment variety: "Most wearable brands have great product shots but can't afford 20 different lifestyle environments."
- Launch angle: "Product launches need more content than you can shoot in a day."
- Performance marketing angle: "AI-generated product content is how you A/B test creative without re-shooting."

## The Ask (always this — nothing bigger)

**Never ask for a call in the first email.** The ask is always low-friction curiosity:

- "Want to see what this looks like for a brand in your space?"
- "Curious if this is relevant to what you're building?"
- "Worth a look?"
- "Happy to put together a quick sample — interested?"

The goal of email 1: get a reply. Not a meeting. Not a demo. A reply.

## Voice Rules for TC Outreach

- Write like a peer practitioner, not a vendor
- "We made this for [similar brand]" beats "We offer AI production services"
- Show don't tell — describe an outcome, not a feature
- Never use: "innovative," "cutting-edge," "leverage," "synergy," "seamless," "game-changing"
- Contractions always. Short sentences.
- Sound like Anant wrote it — direct, confident, zero hedging
