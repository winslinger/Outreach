## TC PITCH

### One-Line
"We make AI-powered commercials for D2C brands — same quality as a studio shoot, built entirely with AI."

### What We Do
- AI video production — lifestyle, mood, product demo, seasonal campaigns
- AI photo production — product stills, lookbook, lifestyle scenes
- Art direction — full creative direction from brief to final file
- Multi-format delivery — reels, ads, campaign sets, social content packages

### Proof Point (current)
ZinCuTec — German premium metal furniture. 8 AI-powered lifestyle and campaign videos this month. Aman-level aesthetic, done entirely with AI. Reference as "a premium D2C furniture brand we're working with" — do not name client unless Anant says to.

### Pain Points by Industry

**Fashion & Apparel**
- Single shoot day = $5k–$20k+. 4 seasons per year = budget obliterated.
- They shoot 2 days a year but need 365 days of content.
- TC angle: full seasonal campaign — 20–30 campaign-quality images + 2–3 videos — at a fraction of one shoot day.

Opening hooks:
- "Most fashion brands run 4 shoots a year and still never have enough content."
- "Studio shoots are the most expensive part of running a product brand."
- "The brands that win on social publish 10x more content than they can afford to shoot traditionally."

**Gadgets & Wearables**
- Same product needs 20 different environment contexts. Location shoots can't afford it.
- Controlled environments + specific lighting setups = expensive per shoot.
- TC angle: same product in 20 different environments — lifestyle, sport, travel, urban — all at scale.

Opening hooks:
- "Most wearable brands have great product shots but can't afford 20 different lifestyle environments."
- "Product launches need more content than you can shoot in a day."
- "AI-generated product content is how you A/B test creative without re-shooting."

### The Ask (always this — never bigger)
Never ask for a call in email 1. Always low-friction curiosity:
- "Want to see what this looks like for a brand in your space?"
- "Curious if this is relevant to what you're building?"
- "Worth a look?"
- "Happy to put together a quick sample — interested?"

**Goal of email 1: get a reply. Not a meeting. Not a demo. A reply.**

## EMAIL WRITING PRINCIPLES

### Core Rules
- Write like a peer practitioner, not a vendor
- Opening line must come from ACTUAL research on that brand — something you only know from looking at them. Not a generic industry pain point.
- Lead with OUTCOME (lower CAC, more sales, more content at scale) — not METHOD (AI-powered production)
- "You're leaving ROAS on the table" beats "We make AI-powered content"
- Show the outcome, don't list features
- Every sentence must earn its place
- Read it aloud — if it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it
- Would YOU reply to this?

### Email Structure
```
Subject: [2-4 words, lowercase — specific to brand or their situation]

[Opening — specific observation from actual research on THAT brand]
[1-2 sentences — what that gap costs them: wasted budget, thin ROAS, content falling short]
[TC as the fix — outcome first. Proof with specifics.]
[One low-friction CTA]

Anant
```

### Outcome vs Method Framing

| Don't say (method) | Say instead (outcome) |
|---|---|
| "We make AI-powered content" | "More content than a shoot budget can afford" |
| "AI at every stage of production" | "20 environments, one brief, delivered in a week" |
| "Same quality as a studio shoot" | "8 campaign videos for a premium furniture brand in 10 days" |
| "AI photo and video production" | "Lower CAC, more creative to test, no location shoots" |

### ZinCuTec Proof Point — Use This Specific Version
"Just wrapped 8 campaign videos for a premium German furniture brand — Mediterranean interiors, warm light, cinematic quality — delivered in 10 days. Entirely with AI."

Shorter version: "8 campaign videos for a premium furniture brand, 10 days, Aman-level quality — entirely with AI."

### Hard Rules
- Subject: 2-4 words, lowercase, no name, no salesy words
- Body: max 100 words. Often less.
- No em dashes (—) anywhere in the email body. Use a comma or period.
- No "I hope this email finds you well"
- No "My name is Anant and I..."
- No feature lists
- One CTA only
- Sign: "Anant" (sometimes "Anant / TC")

### Banned Words
innovative, cutting-edge, leverage, synergy, seamless, game-changing, streamline, best-in-class, leading provider, circle back

## ALL FRAMEWORKS (full reference)

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution
Identify pain → Amplify consequences → Present solution + soft CTA.
Best for: problem-aware but not solution-aware. The workhorse.

### BAB — Before, After, Bridge
Current painful state → Ideal future → Product as the bridge.
Best for: transformation-driven offers, emotional decision-makers.

### QVC — Question, Value, CTA
Targeted pain question → Brief value → Direct next step.
Best for: C-suite who prefer brevity.

### AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
Hook/stat → specific challenge → social proof → CTA.
Best for: data-driven prospects, high-ticket with strong stats.

### PPP — Praise, Picture, Push
Genuine compliment → how things could be better → gentle push.
Best for: senior prospects who respond to relationships. Requires genuine trigger.

### Star-Story-Solution
Introduce customer character → challenge narrative → results revealed.
Best for: strong customer success stories. Humanises the pitch.

### SCQ — Situation, Complication, Question
Current reality → complicating challenge → question that speaks to need.
Best for: consultative selling, mirrors how professionals present to leadership.

### ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action
Contrarian hook → explain simply → proof → strong CTA.
Best for: analytical buyers (engineers, CFOs, ops).

### Mouse Trap (Lavender / Will Allred)
Observation + binary value-prop question. 1-2 sentences total.
Best for: maximum brevity. Impulsive reply from curiosity.

### 3C's (Alex Berman)
Compliment → Case Study → CTA.
Best for: agency/services cold outreach.

### Justin Michael Method
Trigger/Pain → Solution hint → Binary CTA. 1-3 sentences, no intro.
Best for: high-velocity. Quote their own words from interviews.

### PASTOR (Ray Edwards)
Problem → Amplify → Story → Testimony → Offer → Response.
Best for: multi-email sequences. Each element developed across separate touches.

## SUBJECT LINE RULES

- 2-4 words. Lowercase. No punctuation tricks.
- Specific to their brand or their situation — not a generic category word.
- Look internal — like it came from a colleague, not a vendor.
- **Never** put product pitch, urgency, emojis, or prospect's first name in subject.

**Data:**
- 2-word subject lines = 60% more opens than 5-word
- All-lowercase = highest open rates (Gong, 85M emails)
- First name in subject = 12% fewer replies (signals automation)
- Salesy language = -17.9% opens
- Numbers/percentages = -46% opens

**Three subject line buckets to rotate through:**

1. **Brand-specific** (references their company or product directly):
   `skinnydip content` · `your product shoots` · `vanmoof content` · `[brand] ads`

2. **Observation-based** (sounds like you noticed something):
   `saw your page` · `saw your spring campaign` · `your shoot budget`

3. **Curiosity/peer** (short, no-pitch energy):
   `quick question` · `quick q` · `content volume` · `worth a look`

**Avoid:** Generic category words used as subjects — "ad creative", "lifestyle content", "wearable content" all read like cold outreach at a glance. Make it feel like you looked at them.

## PERSONALIZATION — 4 LEVELS

| Level | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basic (merge tags) | First name, company name — table stakes |
| 2 | Industry/segment | Industry pain points, trends |
| 3 | Role-level | Challenges specific to their seniority |
| 4 | Individual (gold standard) | Specific, timely observation about that person connected to the problem |

**Minimum for TC outreach: Level 4. Always. The agent visits the brand website before writing.**

### Research Signal Stack
- `keywords_short` → what they sell / brand angle → TC's relevant hook
- `revenue` + `employees` → company scale → content volume needs
- `industry` → correct TC pitch angle
- `city/country` → localize tone if relevant
- `website` → brand aesthetic signals

### The "So What?" Test
After writing any opening line, read from prospect's perspective: "So what? Why would I care?" If the answer is nothing, rewrite.

### Breakup Email (1-2-3 Format)
> Since I haven't heard back, I'll keep it simple. Reply with a number:
> 1 — Interested, let's talk
> 2 — Not now, check back in 3 months
> 3 — Not interested, please stop

**If you send a breakup email, honor it. Do not contact the prospect again.**

### Phrases That Kill Response Rates
- "I never heard back" → -12% meeting booking rate
- "Just checking in" → zero value
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- "Did you see my last email?"
- "Following up on my previous message"

## COLD MODELS — COURSE FRAMEWORKS (Akshat's own system)

*These are the foundational mental models from the Cold Models PDF course. Apply to TC outreach strategy.*

### Ch 2 — Awareness Levels

80% of people in any market are unaware they have a problem. Target the sweet spot.

| Level | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Unaware | Don't know they have a problem | Energy suckers — not good targets |
| 2 — Problem-Aware | Know they have a problem, exploring options | Good — they don't need convincing |
| 3 — Solution-Aware | Posting/doing consistently but can't grow/convert | **SWEET SPOT — start here** |
| 4 — Growing + can't convert to money | Sophisticated — educated, know the work | Harder to sell to but easier to close if you can |
| 5 — Growing + can't automate/scale | Most sophisticated segment | They'll pay easily but will call BS immediately |

**For TC at this stage: target Awareness Level 3.** They're sold on the problem (need better content/ads), not deep enough to know everything, and hungry for a solution.

**Content strategy by awareness level:** Different messaging for each level. One template cannot serve all.

### Ch 3 — Solution / Opportunity

**The fundamental rule:** You either sell FOMO on an opportunity or you solve a painful problem. "Feel good services" don't sell.

**Screening test for TC's offer:**
1. If I had £1M in the bank and someone pitched me this service at this price — would I buy it? (Yes — good ads directly boost an ecom brand)
2. Would I reply to my own email?

**The core shift:** Don't pitch "good looking videos." Pitch what good videos *get them* — lower CAC, higher ROAS, more qualified leads, brand trust. Clients don't care about the output quality. They care about the result the output delivers.

**TC application:** Frame every email around the commercial result (more sales, lower CAC, more content at scale) — not around AI, not around production quality.

### Ch 3 — Value Equation (Hormozi framework)

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

**The ideal offer = numerator at max, denominator at zero.**

| Variable | What it means | How to increase for TC |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | What they ultimately want | More sales, lower CAC, brand authority — all reduce to: making more money |
| Perceived Likelihood | Do they believe you'll deliver? | ZinCuTec proof, show the work publicly, portfolio samples |
| Time Delay | How long until they see results | "Campaign ready in 7 days" — bound promises to timeframes |
| Effort & Sacrifice | What they have to do | "You brief us, we handle everything" — zero client effort in production |

**Beginners Misery problem:** Without proof of work, you can't credibly integrate all 4 pillars. Solution: use ZinCuTec as the proof point immediately.

**Dream outcomes always reduce to 5 things:** Making more money · Saving time · Adding to their status · Improving health · Improving relationships. For D2C brands: #1 is money, #3 is status.

### Ch 5 — Free Offers (for early-stage client acquisition — pre-TC, but relevant context)

**Key principles:**
- Free is not actually free — it costs a founder their time and energy
- Offering for free lowers perceived value
- Free offer goal = testimonials and case studies, NOT money
- The 3-step free offer framework:
  1. Get really good at what you're selling — if you don't believe you can help someone, don't sell
  2. Build a portfolio of personal projects — have 5 examples that will absolutely blow their mind
  3. Give free/low-ticket deliverables with believable claims → goal is testimonials + case studies
- **TC is past this stage** — ZinCuTec is the case study. No free work needed.

### Ch 6 — One Time Offers (OTO)

**Why OTOs matter:** Cold prospects won't commit to 3–6 month retainers immediately. OTOs lower the commitment barrier, build trust, and let you demonstrate results before the bigger ask.

**Creating a banger OTO — 5 rules:**
1. **Solve a painful problem or present a big opportunity** — sell painkillers, not vitamins. Pain killers always make more money.
2. **Talk about tangible results** — "I will get you 10 qualified sales calls in 30 days using our systems or you don't pay." Make the result specific and believable. 30 days is a good timeframe.
3. **Make delivery time fast** — reduce time between payment and delivery. Automate/templatize. Onboarding call immediately after payment.
4. **Offer a money-back guarantee if results aren't delivered** — put it in the contract, not just in words. Only give to prospects you're 80%+ confident you can deliver for. Don't give to random people.
5. **Reduce resistance** — make it effortless for them. If they have to invest time and energy after paying, it's a deal breaker.

**TC OTO idea:** A single 3-video package (or 5-image campaign set) delivered in 7 days. Fixed fee. Money-back if not Aman-level quality. Brief us, we handle everything.
